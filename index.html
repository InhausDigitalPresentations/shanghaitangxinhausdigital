The question

What if an opening film moved at two speeds? The calm of dressing for dinner. The rush of a restaurant getting ready.

Classic. Refined. Rooted in fine dining.

The lens

Fashion is how we enter.

The wardrobe comes directly from Shanghai Tang in China. The clothes come first, and the restaurant is discovered through them.

The idea

Before a great dinner, everyone gets ready.

She dresses in Shanghai Tang, slowly.

The restaurant gets ready for her, fast.

A fashion film that moves between the calm of a ritual and the speed of a kitchen.

Two tempos

Slow.

The ritual. Locked off. Long takes.

Fast.

The preparation. Precise. Rhythmic.

Getting ready

She takes her time.

Silk. Buttons. Earrings. Lipstick.

The edit

The ritual, cut against the rush.

Long, still takes of her getting ready. Bursts of a few frames from the kitchen and the dining room. The faster they move, the more precious her evening feels.

Frame widths drawn to shot length

Meanwhile, the restaurant

Everything
else moves fast.

Mise en place

Fast, never chaotic.

The kitchen and the dining room move like choreography. Speed ramps, match cuts, clean precise moves.

The table

The knife

The fold

The room is ready. The pace drops at once.

They arrive. Everything slows.

The dinner

Course by course, at her pace.

Each course is a short burst back into the kitchen, then the dish lands in calm. At the table, the brief's three territories come together.

The pause

Mid-dinner, she steps away.

The powder room. She retouches her lips. The quietest moment of the film.

Match cut

One stroke. Two rituals.

Her lipstick cuts to a chef's brush of glaze. Then back to the table.

The last course arrives. A look between them.

Back to the evening.
